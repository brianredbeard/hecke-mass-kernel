# Finite double-coset sums in the formalization

Reciprocal-stabilizer masses and Hecke sums over coset representatives are classical. Gross uses both in the Brandt-module computation at level 11. Kirschmer and Voight use the mass sum in ideal-class enumeration, and Greenberg and Voight give the finite coset-sum formula for a Hecke operator. Our contribution is identifying a shared finite-table primitive in a distinctive configuration inside Alpöge’s formalization, extracting the arithmetic, and tracing how its two forms are used in the proof rooted at `riemannHypothesis`. [Gross, §6](https://wstein.org/papers/bib/Gross-Heights_and_the_Special_Values_of_L-series.pdf) · [Kirschmer–Voight, Remark 7.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0808.3833) · [Greenberg–Voight, §2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1209.2460) · [Frozen proof root](https://github.com/homeowmorphism/riemann-hypothesis-original/blob/4e8bb36faa8c9e3f1bda144ccf5a637f8b96066e/src/Main.lean#L4)

## The primitive

Both uses reduce a double-coset calculation to a sum over a supplied table. The mass table has one row per class in $\Gamma\backslash G/K$, weighted by the reciprocal of its stabilizer order. The Hecke table has one row per coset $\gamma J$ in $J\alpha J$, weighted by the value $F(x\gamma)$. For a place of norm $q$, degree $k$, rank $r$ and character value $u$, the Hecke sum is scaled by $u^{-1}(q^{k\lfloor r/2\rfloor})^{-1}$. The kernel evaluates the finite rational specialization. Its interpretation in the original definitions requires certified representatives and row values. ## Where the proof uses it

The compiled dependency audit follows declaration bodies from `riemannHypothesis` through `rhOfAll` and `allChi` to the universal character nonvanishing result. The two paths share the passage from theta integrals and local matching to definite-side existence, then use the arithmetic in different arguments. [Frozen proof root](https://github.com/homeowmorphism/riemann-hypothesis-original/blob/4e8bb36faa8c9e3f1bda144ccf5a637f8b96066e/src/Main.lean#L4) · [Passage from theta matching to `EveryCharacter`](https://github.com/homeowmorphism/riemann-hypothesis-original/blob/4e8bb36faa8c9e3f1bda144ccf5a637f8b96066e/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/ThetaCorrespondence/ThetaPairLifts/ThirdPlaceSeries/SiegelEisensteinSecondTermHypotheses.lean#L69-L74)

- **Mass identifies a volume.** `volumes_eq_masses_of_unfold` uses the definite double-coset decomposition to obtain a volume–mass identity. The dependency path reaches `classMass` through `doubleCosets_mass` and `defClassDecomp`. Once representatives and true stabilizer orders are given, the mass side of that identity is the reciprocal sum. The audited body dependency path has 15 edges. [Volume–mass theorem](https://github.com/homeowmorphism/riemann-hypothesis-original/blob/4e8bb36faa8c9e3f1bda144ccf5a637f8b96066e/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/IwasawaDecomposition/VolumesEqMassesOfUnfold.lean#L12-L19)
- **The Hecke sum specifies a readout.** `HeckeAt` requires the readout of a target form $g$ to equal the normalized Hecke sum applied to the readout of $f$, for every certified local double-coset datum. `heckeTieT` transports this compatibility to the copied setup used in the series construction. The audited body dependency path through `heckeTieT` and `HeckeAt` to `Setup.hecke` has 19 edges. [Transport theorem](https://github.com/homeowmorphism/riemann-hypothesis-original/blob/4e8bb36faa8c9e3f1bda144ccf5a637f8b96066e/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Proofs/IwasawaDecomposition/ClassSumEq.lean#L206-L208) · [Hecke readout condition](https://github.com/homeowmorphism/riemann-hypothesis-original/blob/4e8bb36faa8c9e3f1bda144ccf5a637f8b96066e/src/Development/AbelianVarietyTheory/Definitions/NonGaloisDescent/AdmQAdmissibleConjugatePairPlaces.lean#L79-L85)

The audited closure has 21 declaration bodies referring to `classMass` and 12 referring to `Setup.hecke`. The exact paths and edge kinds are recorded in `proof_profile/research/spine-01/spine_milestones.json` in the workbench source tree. The paths establish dependency through the compiled Lean declarations. The standalone examples illustrate the arithmetic on explicit tables. Orders 4 and 6 give mass $5/12$. Two Hecke samples 2 and 4 with $u=-1$, $q=3$, $k=1$ and $r=3$ give $-2$. Lean, C, and Rust replay both examples.
