# Prior art for finite Hecke arithmetic

The level-11 Brandt matrix and the two Gaussian genus masses have exact matches in earlier publications. Earlier Hermitian mass formulas also give the cyclotomic values, and a published classification includes the standard rank-three cyclotomic lattice. The prime-pair filter searches a classical arithmetic progression, with close precedents for its prescribed divisors in CSIDH and CSURF. The workbench’s most specific contribution lies in the evidence it packages around those calculations. It combines quaternion-neighbor witnesses, Lean statements with explicit premises, and independent arithmetic checks. ## Gross published the same level-11 matrix in 1987

Gross prints **$B(2)=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&2\\3&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)$** in the same class order as the workbench. He defines the weights as unit group orders modulo $\{\pm1\}$. His weights $2,3$ therefore correspond to full orders $4,6$ and mass $1/4+1/6=5/12$. That mass is also the constant term in his Eisenstein series. Gross gives both the normalized eta product $\eta(\tau)^2\eta(11\tau)^2$ and the model $y^2+y=x^3-x^2-10x-20$ of $X_0(11)$. Cremona’s tables cover the Frobenius traces at primes below $100$, and the LMFDB links the curve to newform 11.2.a.a. [Gross, §§1 and 6, pp. 146–147](https://wstein.org/papers/bib/Gross-Heights_and_the_Special_Values_of_L-series.pdf) · [Cremona, Appendix to Chapter II](https://wstein.org/edu/Fall2003/252/references/cremona-algs/Cremona-Algorithms_for_modular_elliptic_curves.pdf) · [LMFDB curve](https://www.lmfdb.org/EllipticCurve/Q/11/a/2) · [LMFDB newform](https://www.lmfdb.org/ModularForm/GL2/Q/holomorphic/11/2/a/a/)

For this review, independently expanding the eta product and counting points on that curve matched **all 24 reported good-prime eigenvalues through 97**. At $p=2$ the curve has five points, giving $a_2=-2$, and the matrix has eigenvalues $3$ and $-2$. Gross’s row sums and weighted symmetry also recover the whole matrix from $a_p$ in this two-class example. Its off-diagonal entries are $2(p+1-a_p)/5$ and $3(p+1-a_p)/5$ in his convention. The workbench reports replaying **2,146** neighbor certificates, consistent with $2\sum_p(p+1)$ neighbors across the two classes. This review checked the expected count and numerical outputs. The individual lattice witnesses and release integrity checks are attributed to the workbench record. [Gross, Proposition 2.7 and Eq. ## Prior implementations already use neighbors and mass stopping

Pizer published an algorithm for quaternionic Hecke matrices in 1980. Sage’s Brandt module documentation gives the three explicit $p=2$ right-ideal neighbors at level 11, and Magma documents its Brandt module implementation. Kirschmer and Voight give quaternion ideal-class enumeration by neighbors and explicitly propose stopping when the accumulated reciprocal unit weights match the independently known mass. Greenberg and Voight give Hecke evaluation as a finite sum over a disjoint coset decomposition and record group elements that identify neighbors with class representatives. Those precedents cover the mathematical operations behind the finite kernel. Its specific normalization $u^{-1}(q^{k\lfloor r/2\rfloor})^{-1}$, conventions at zero, and Lean/C/Rust interface define the contract of this package. [Pizer, 1980](https://doi.org/10.1016/0021-8693(80)90151-9) · [Sage Brandt modules](https://doc.sagemath.org/html/en/reference/modfrm/sage/modular/quatalg/brandt.html) · [Magma Brandt modules](https://www.math.uzh.ch/sepp/magma-2.19.3-cr/html/text1551.htm) · [Kirschmer–Voight, Remarks 5.2 and 7.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0808.3833) · [Greenberg–Voight, §§2 and 6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1209.2460) · [Kernel specification](../docs/specification.md)

The LMFDB authors cross-checked modular-symbol data against explicit trace formulas, and Birkbeck’s Lean project formalizes Hecke rings and coefficient relations in a strong multiplicity one development. Dang’s 2026 Richelot–Brandt work provides a genus-two analogue with archived exact spectral certificates and a separate replay. Within this search, the more specific combination was the workbench’s level-11 per-neighbor evidence, Lean-checked conditional stopping statement, and point-count and eta-product comparisons. The specialization of the quaternion mass and Brandt correspondence to the adelic formalization remains open in the workbench record. The standalone repository’s Lean proofs and C/Rust fixture tests apply to the finite arithmetic on supplied tables. ## The prime filter searches a classical progression inside certified windows

For fixed $q$, set $M=q\prod_{3\le p\le B,\ p\ \mathrm{prime}}p$. Since $M$ is odd, $M\mid d+1$ and $d\equiv7\pmod{16}$ give the single reduced progression **$d\equiv 8M-1\pmod{16M}$**. Dirichlet’s theorem, already formalized in Mathlib, supplies primes in that class above any lower bound. With upper bounds removed, one can first choose an admissible $q$ and then choose $d$ beyond $q$ and the prime divisors of $N$. Linnik-type bounds concern a least prime in a reduced class. The checker addresses a different quantifier by testing a pair inside two specified windows. [Dirichlet’s memoir](https://arxiv.org/abs/0808.1408) · [Mathlib, primes in arithmetic progressions](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/NumberTheory/LSeries/PrimesInAP.html#Nat.forall_exists_prime_gt_and_modEq) · [Xylouris, 2018](https://www.mathnet.ru/php/archive.phtml?jrnid=cheb&option_lang=eng&paperid=681&wshow=paper) · [Prime-filter concept card](../proof_profile/research/batch-03/concepts/qh-prime-filter.md)

**CSIDH-512 already uses a field prime with $p+1$ divisible by an initial block of odd primes and one larger designated prime.** CSURF-512 uses $p+1=8$ times a prescribed odd product, hence the same $p\equiv7\pmod{16}$ condition as the workbench imposes on $d$. The workbench also imposes $q\equiv3\pmod8$, coprimality with $N$, and separate bounded windows. Its prime list specifies divisors of $d+1$, which include a prime strictly greater than $B$. [CSIDH, 2018](https://eprint.iacr.org/2018/383) · [Castryck–Decru, CSURF](https://biblio.ugent.be/publication/8665496) · [Bernstein et al., §6](https://msp.org/obs/2020/4-1/obs-v4-n1-p04-s.pdf)

Bröker explicitly gives $y^2=x^3-x$ as a CM curve with supersingular reduction for primes $p\equiv3\pmod4$. In this model, replacing $x$ by $-x$ cancels the quadratic character sum, yielding $\#E(\mathbf F_p)=p+1$. An independent exhaustive count confirmed **7,080** points over $\mathbf F_{7079}$ and 60 over $\mathbf F_{59}$. Trial division also confirmed the pair and the five benchmark examples. Primality certificate replay already exists in Lean and Isabelle. The workbench’s specific proof ties the executable filter to certified integer floors of a real window and supplies that named pair as a witness. This review used independent arithmetic checks and relies on the workbench record for the Lean/Python suite. The full analytic PairGood decision remains open. [Bröker, §2](https://gkorpal.github.io/files/broker-supersingular.pdf) · [Mathlib primality tactic](https://leanprover-community.github.io/mathlib4_docs/Mathlib/Tactic/Primality.html) · [Isabelle’s Pratt certificate formalization](https://isa-afp.org/entries/Pratt_Certificate.html) · [Window witness](../proof_profile/research/batch-03/benchmarks/qh-prime-filter/WindowWitness.lean) · [Prime-filter benchmark](../proof_profile/research/batch-03/benchmarks/qh-prime-filter/review.md)

## Earlier mass formulas and tables cover the Hermitian examples

The unramified split and inert factors $q^m/(q^m-1)$ and $q^m/(q^m-(-1)^m)$ are the rank-$m$ Euler terms of the standard unitary mass formula, with $q$ the base residue cardinality. For the split count, each nonzero $x$ admits $q^{m-1}$ vectors $y$ with $x\cdot y=1$. At an inert place the classical Hermitian unit-vector count is $q^{m-1}(q^m-(-1)^m)$. Taking the adjacent-rank quotient cancels lower-rank factors. The workbench represents a split conjugation orbit by its combined rational base-place factor. For $m\ge2$, its tail interval follows by bounding each omitted factor and its reciprocal by $(1-p^{-2})^{-1}$ above a rational prime $p$ and telescoping over all integers beyond a complete cutoff. The classical formula also includes bad-place corrections, which enter the absolute masses. [Gan–Hanke–Yu, §§2 and 4](https://web.math.princeton.edu/~jonhanke/Web-02/Mass-Formula-of-Shimura/shimura-mass.pdf) · [Kirschmer, Proposition 4.2.7](https://www.math.uni-bielefeld.de/~mkirschm/papers/herm.pdf) · [Dumas–Gow–Sheekey, Lemma 2](https://membres-ljk.imag.fr/Jean-Guillaume.Dumas/Publications/symmetricrank.pdf) · [Workbench absolute-mass notes](../proof_profile/research/batch-03/mining/absolute-mass/mined_proof_notes.md)

Schiemann’s published Gaussian tables give the **exact masses $1/32$ and $1/384$** for the standard rank-two and rank-three genera. Jürgens and Zimmermann explicitly give the odd-lattice dyadic correction $1-2^{-m}$ for even rank in their normalization. This yields the workbench’s $3/4$ at rank two. [Schiemann, Gaussian rank two](https://www.math.uni-sb.de/ag/schulze/Hermitian-lattices/D-4/dim2) · [Schiemann, Gaussian rank three](https://www.math.uni-sb.de/ag/schulze/Hermitian-lattices/D-4/dim3) · [Jürgens–Zimmermann, Eq. (5) and Corollary 4.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1512.03297#page=14)

For $\mathbf Z[\zeta_{12}]^m$, exact rational evaluation of earlier formulas gives **$1/12,\ 1/288,\ 1/10368$** in ranks one through three, with ratios $1/24$ and $1/36$. Put $F=\mathbf Q(\sqrt3)$ and $E=\mathbf Q(\zeta_{12})$. The field discriminants give relative discriminant norm $144/12^2=1$, so the standard unimodular lattice has unit local corrections in Kirschmer’s formula. Biquadratic factorization and finite Bernoulli sums give $L_F(0,\eta)=1/6$, $\zeta_F(-1)=1/6$, and $L_F(-2,\eta)=1/9$, where $\eta$ is the quadratic character of $E/F$. Multiplying the rank-indexed factors with $2^{1-2m}$ gives the stated masses. Mureau gives a directly applicable standard cyclotomic rank-two formula. Kirschmer’s classification explicitly lists the identity Gram matrix for this field among the rank-three one-class genera. The matching rank-three value follows by weighting that class by the reciprocal of its full automorphism order $12^3\cdot3!$. This search located the formula and classification entry, with the two numerical fractions obtained by specialization. ## The evidence supports a claim about reproducibility

Mass-based stopping is already the default for Magma’s Hermitian genus enumeration, and Hecke.jl computes exact rational masses and genus representatives. A portable stopping certificate can expose exactly what a consumer must establish about the list, its stabilizers, and the total mass. For the Brandt calculation, per-neighbor evidence adds a way to audit how each matrix entry arose even when the final matrix is recoverable from a published coefficient. The search found comparable methods and workflows and leaves priority for the exact combination unresolved.
